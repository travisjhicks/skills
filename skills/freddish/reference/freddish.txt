Step 1. State the idea you wish to express as clearly as possible, and in terms preschoolers can understand.
Example: "It is dangerous to play in the street."

Step 2. Take a statement intended for preschoolers and rephrase it in a positive manner.
Before: "It is dangerous to play in the street."
After: "It is good to play where it is safe."

Step 3. Take a statement intended for preschoolers and rephrase it bearing in mind that preschoolers cannot yet make subtle distinctions and need to be redirected to authorities they trust.
Before: "It is good to play where it is safe."
After: "Ask your parents where it is safe to play."

Step 4. Take a statement intended for preschoolers and rephrase it to eliminate all elements that could be considered prescriptive, directive, or instructive.
Before: "Ask your parents where it is safe to play."
After: "Your parents will tell you where it is safe to play."

Step 5. Take a statement intended for preschoolers and rephrase any element that suggests certainty.
Before: "Your parents will tell you where it is safe to play."
After: "Your parents can tell you where it is safe to play."

Step 6. Take a statement intended for preschoolers and rephrase it to eliminate any element that may not apply to all children. (For instance, not all children know their parents.)
Before: "Your parents can tell you where it is safe to play."
After: "Your favorite grown-ups can tell you where it is safe to play."

Step 7. Take a statement intended for preschoolers and add a simple motivational idea that gives preschoolers a reason to follow the advice.
Before: "Your favorite grown-ups can tell you where it is safe to play."
After: "Your favorite grown-ups can tell you where it is safe to play. It is good to listen to them."

Step 8. Take a statement intended for preschoolers and rephrase it to be as clear as possible, in terms preschoolers can understand. In particular, remove any words that represent value judgments (such as "good").
Before: "Your favorite grown-ups can tell you where it is safe to play. It is good to listen to them."
After: "Your favorite grown-ups can tell you where it is safe to play. It is important to try to listen to them."

Step 9. Take a statement intended for preschoolers and rephrase it to relate the idea to some phase of development a preschooler can understand.
Before: "Your favorite grown-ups can tell you where it is safe to play. It is important to try to listen to them."
After: "Your favorite grown-ups can tell you where it is safe to play. It is important to try to listen to them, and listening is an important part of growing."
